Why Is My Instagram Reach So Low? The Instagram Marketing Truth Nobody's Posting About

Somewhere in your content calendar is a post that used to get 4,000 views. You posted the same format last week. It got 380.

You didn't get worse at this. Instagram made a change bigger than a "tweak," and it happened further back than most brands think. A lot of content calendars are still running an instagram marketing playbook built for a platform that stopped existing well over a year ago.

Let's decode what actually happened, because the real answer isn't "post more" or "the algorithm hates you." It's more specific than that, and more fixable.


The Quick Answer

Your Instagram reach is low because Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, confirmed back in January 2025 that ranking is primarily based on three signals, in this order: watch time, sends per reach (DM shares), and likes per reach. That hierarchy has remained consistent and even strengthened through 2026.

Watch time leads. Sends are the strongest signal for reaching people who don't already follow you. Likes still count, but mostly for people who already know you.

Here's the part that should sting a little: most brand content calendars are still built to farm likes. Punchy one-liners. Quote graphics. "Double tap if you agree." That content isn't wrong, it's just optimising for the metric Instagram now cares about least.


The Metric Most Brand Managers Have Never Opened

Open your Instagram Insights right now. There's a number called Shares. Almost nobody on the marketing side checks it.

A DM share is a different kind of action than a like. Liking takes half a second and means almost nothing about intent. Sending something to one specific friend means you had to think of that exact person and decide they needed to see this. That's a signal Instagram can't fake, buy, or bot its way around, which is exactly why the system leans on it so heavily. A few independent industry analyses put a DM share at roughly three to five times the distribution value of a like. Instagram hasn't published an exact multiplier, so treat that ratio as a directional estimate, not an official figure, but the direction itself is consistent across sources.

The practical shift: stop asking "will people like this?" Start asking "would someone send this to one specific friend, and could I name that friend?" If you can't picture the person, the post won't travel.


The Feature Quietly Working Against You

Instagram added a setting most brands don't know exists: a content preferences dashboard where users can directly tell Instagram which topics to stop showing them. It's not a ranking signal you can win with better content. If someone has opted out of your category, your post can perform beautifully and still never reach them.

This matters for instagram marketing strategy because it means "make better content" isn't the whole answer anymore. Niche clarity is. The more precisely defined your content category is, the more accurately Instagram can match you to people who haven't opted out, and to new audiences who'd actually opt in.


Reposting Is Quietly Taxing Your Reach

If your content pipeline is "repurpose the TikTok, slap it on Reels," that habit is now working against you. Original content gets meaningfully more distribution than reposted or watermarked content, and accounts that repost heavily risk being excluded from recommendations altogether, not just deprioritised. Instagram hasn't published the exact thresholds it uses, but the direction is consistent across independent analyses: cross-posted, watermarked content is now a measurable drag on reach, not the neutral time-saver it used to be.

A lot of brands built their entire "efficient content strategy" around cross-posting. In 2026, efficient and effective stopped being the same word.

Business Accounts Start the Race Already Behind

Here's a fact almost no one puts in the pitch deck: business accounts have a lower baseline organic reach than personal accounts, even before you post a single thing. It's a structural gap, not a performance problem. It doesn't mean business accounts can't win. It means the signals above (shares, watch time, original content) aren't optional extras for brands. They're how you close a gap that exists by default.


The Hook Window Is Shorter Than "3 Seconds"

The old advice was "hook them in the first three seconds." That number still gets repeated everywhere, but it's increasingly treated as the outer edge, not the target. A figure that circulates a lot in industry write-ups puts the real decision window closer to two seconds, though it doesn't trace back to any study Instagram has actually published, so treat the exact number skeptically. What's not in dispute: if your Reel opens with a logo animation or a slow zoom-in, you've likely lost a meaningful chunk of your audience before your idea even starts.


Hashtags Aren't Dead. They're Just Decorative Now

Instagram removed the ability to follow hashtags back in late 2024, and their role in discovery has been shrinking ever since. Three to five specific, relevant hashtags still offer a small assist. Thirty generic ones do nothing except make your caption look like a ransom note.


What Creative Nerds Would Actually Do About It

Not a listicle of hacks. Here's what the team at Creative Nerds actually tells clients once reach starts sliding:

  • Design one post a week specifically to be sent, not liked. Ask "who is the one friend this is for?"
  • Pull your Shares number in Insights before you pull your Likes number
  • Cut cross-posted, watermarked content from your calendar, or re-edit it into something native
  • Get sharper on your niche instead of broader. Vague content can't be matched to the right audience
  • Rewrite your first line and your first frame. You're not fighting for three seconds anymore, you're fighting for two
  • Stop stuffing hashtags. Pick five that actually describe the post

Strategy before aesthetics, always. A gorgeous Reel built on 2024 assumptions still underperforms an average one built on how the algorithm actually works today.


Conclusion

Instagram's marketing rules have changed faster than most content calendars. The brands catching up today aren't posting more, they're posting for the right signals.

That's the real job. Understand how the platform works, create content that aligns with what it's actually measuring, and stop relying on guesswork.

This is where many brands still think too narrowly. They treat the blog as the centre of gravity, when in reality discovery now happens across an ecosystem.
